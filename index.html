What Are Coral Reefs?

Coral reefs are underwater ecosystems characterized by reef-building corals. Reefs are formed of colonies of coral polyps held together by calcium carbonate. Most coral reefs are built from stony corals, whose polyps cluster in groups.

Often called \"rainforests of the sea,\" coral reefs are among the most biologically diverse and valuable ecosystems on Earth. They occupy less than 1% of the ocean floor but are home to more than 25% of all marine species.

Corals are actually tiny animals called polyps that belong to the same group as jellyfish and anemones. Polyps live in symbiosis with photosynthetic algae called zooxanthellae, which provide the coral with food and give them their vibrant colors.

The Incredible Diversity of Coral Reefs

Coral reefs are among the most diverse ecosystems on the planet, supporting an extraordinary variety of marine life.

Major Types of Coral Reefs

Fringing Reefs

These reefs grow near the coastline around islands and continents. They are separated from the shore by narrow, shallow lagoons. Fringing reefs are the most common type of reef.

Barrier Reefs

These reefs are separated from land by a lagoon of open, often deep water. The Great Barrier Reef in Australia is the largest and most famous barrier reef in the world.

Atolls

These are rings of coral that create protected lagoons and are usually located in the middle of the sea. Atolls usually form when islands surrounded by fringing reefs sink into the sea or the sea level rises around them.

Incredible Marine Biodiversity

Coral Species

Scientists have identified approximately 800 species of reef-building corals and thousands of other soft corals, invertebrates, and algae species.

Reef Fish

More than 4,000 species of fish make their home in coral reefs, including angelfish, clownfish, parrotfish, and many others that contribute to the reef's vibrant ecosystem.

Invertebrates

Thousands of invertebrate species like crustaceans, mollusks, sponges, and echinoderms thrive in the complex reef environment, each playing a unique role in the ecosystem.

Essential Components of Reef Ecosystems

Coral Polyps

These tiny animals are the building blocks of coral reefs. Each polyp has a sac-like body and an opening, or mouth, encircled by stinging tentacles. They extract calcium from seawater and create a hard, cup-shaped skeleton of limestone around themselves for protection.

Zooxanthellae

These microscopic algae live in the coral's tissues and have a mutually beneficial relationship with coral polyps. Through photosynthesis, they provide the coral with oxygen and nutrients, removing waste products at the same time. They are also responsible for the vibrant colors of many corals.

Symbiotic Relationships

Coral reef ecosystems thrive on complex relationships between different organisms. These symbiotic relationships are crucial for the survival and health of the reef.

Coral and Zooxanthellae: The Foundation of Reefs

+

The most important symbiotic relationship in coral reef ecosystems is between coral polyps and zooxanthellae algae. This is a mutualistic relationship where both organisms benefit:

  • Zooxanthellae provide corals with oxygen and up to 90% of their energy needs through photosynthesis
  • Corals provide the algae with protection and compounds needed for photosynthesis
  • This relationship allows corals to grow and build reefs in nutrient-poor tropical waters
  • When ocean temperatures rise, this relationship can break down resulting in coral bleaching

Cleaner Fish and Their Clients

+

Cleaner fish and shrimp provide a valuable service to larger fish on the reef:

  • They remove parasites, dead skin, and other debris from larger \"client\" fish
  • The cleaners get a meal, while the clients get cleaned of harmful parasites
  • Cleaner stations on the reef are recognized by many fish species who wait their turn for cleaning
  • This relationship demonstrates how cooperation can evolve in complex ecosystems

Clownfish and Sea Anemones

+

One of the most famous symbiotic relationships in coral reefs is between clownfish and sea anemones:

  • Clownfish live among the venomous tentacles of sea anemones, protected from the anemone's sting by a mucus layer
  • The clownfish defends the anemone from predators and parasites
  • The fish's waste provides nutrients for the anemone
  • The anemone provides protection for the clownfish from predators

Coral Reef Zones

Coral reefs have distinct zones with different environmental conditions, supporting various types of coral and marine life.

Reef Zonation Profile

Reef Flat

The shallow platform between the shoreline and reef crest. This area experiences significant wave action, variable temperatures, and can be exposed during low tides. Typically dominated by more robust coral species and algae.
